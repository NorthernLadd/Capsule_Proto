# Initial brainstorming apps

## 1 *"Moddit"* 
### - This app is a social media app where users can post their cars and any modifications that the user has done to the car

### App Evaluation
- **Category:** Social networking / Automotive
- **Mobile:** Users can take pictures of their cars and modifications with their phone and upload them directly. Notifications can alert users to comments and likes on their posts.
- **Story:** Car enthusiasts can document their builds, share the modifications they have made, and find inspiration from other users' cars.
- **Market:** The intended audience is car enthusiasts, especially people who modify their cars or want ideas for future builds. A community focused on a single car model could be a useful starting audience.
- **Habit:** Users could browse builds regularly, interact with other owners, and post updates whenever they make a modification. Browsing may happen more often than posting because modifications take time and money.
- **Scope:** A first version could include accounts, car profiles, photo posts with modification descriptions, a feed, likes, and comments. Parts marketplaces, direct messaging, and detailed build cost tracking could be added later.

## 2 *"Debattle"*
### - This app is a debate grader app, where users can highlight debates or articles line by line and grade each line by whether it was a good or bad point

### App Evaluation
- **Category:** Education / Discussion
- **Mobile:** Users can read articles, select passages, and rate arguments on their phone during short study sessions. Sharing an article link into the app could make it easier to start an evaluation. Furthermore the app can function as a microphone during live debates and record the debate for future grading
- **Story:** Users can practice critical thinking by breaking an argument into individual points, rating their strength, and explaining their reasoning. Ratings reflect users' opinions rather than verified measures of accuracy.
- **Market:** The intended audience is students, debate club members, and readers who want to examine arguments more carefully. A classroom or debate club could provide an initial group of users.
- **Habit:** Users could return for class assignments, debate practice, or articles they want to analyze. Regular use would depend on having new material and a reason to review other users' evaluations.
- **Scope:** A first version could let users paste text, select individual lines, mark points as good or bad, add explanations, and save evaluations. Automatic article importing, video debate transcripts, and shared grading would increase complexity and could be added later.

## 3 *"Hobbiest"*
### - This app is a social media app where people can post their hobbies. It is split into games, books, music, and movies

### App Evaluation
- **Category:** Social networking / Entertainment
- **Mobile:** Users can quickly post photos or short updates about their hobbies and browse category feeds from their phone. Notifications can bring them back when someone responds to a post.
- **Story:** People can share what they are playing, reading, watching, or listening to and connect with others who have similar interests.
- **Market:** The intended audience is people interested in games, books, music, or movies who want a place to discuss their interests. Starting with a campus community could help users find relevant conversations.
- **Habit:** Users could check feeds daily for recommendations and discussions, then post when they start or finish a book, game, movie, or album. Following users and receiving comments could encourage repeat visits.
- **Scope:** A first version could include accounts, text and photo posts, the four category feeds, likes, and comments. Personalized recommendations, private groups, and integrations with entertainment services could be added later.
